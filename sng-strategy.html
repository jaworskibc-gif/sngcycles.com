Overview
Six-Phase Road to Market

The SNG Motors commercialization strategy is built around a single principle: generate real-world proof before committing capital to a production run. One frame. One build. Documented in public. Off-road demonstrated. Pre-orders taken. Then 30 frames — funded by committed buyers before the first unit ships.

This document covers the complete execution blueprint: corporate architecture, import logistics, dual powertrain strategy, content and demo strategy, pre-order mechanics, and the full street-legal manufacturing pathway including NHTSA filing and VIN assignment.

The Core Thesis

Import one frame. Build it live on social media. Demo it off-road. Collect deposits. Use deposits to fund 30-unit street-legal production run. Compliance is handled with customer money already in the account — not investor capital.

Phase 1
Corporate Architecture & Patent Shielding

Before revealing prototypes or accepting customer capital, the corporate structure must isolate liability to protect personal wealth and the three core patents.

The Asset Shield — Dual-LLC Structure
Entity A
IP Holding LLC
Holds sole ownership of the 3 core electric vehicle utility patents. Does no active manufacturing or selling. Patents are completely untouchable from product liability exposure.
Entity B
Manufacturing & Operations LLC
Handles imports, assembly workflows, marketing campaigns, and customer deposits. Executes a formal license agreement with Entity A to use the patents. If a product liability suit targets the manufacturing line, Entity B absorbs it — patents remain protected.

Phase 2
Single-Frame Import & Assembly Logistics

Bypass heavy federal customs and corporate vehicle manufacturer requirements by importing a single engineering sample frame.

Customs Declaration

Instruct the Chinese factory to ship one unbranded 4130 Chromoly frame via air freight (DHL or FedEx). The customs manifest must explicitly read: "Prototype metal tubing for off-road recreation equipment evaluation." Customs will clear this as a raw material component without triggering vehicle import bonds.

Thermal Assembly Execution

Follow the precision thermal press-fit methodology. Freeze the heavy-duty tapered roller steering bearings to shrink them microscopically. Heat the chromoly head tube neck uniformly under 400°F to expand it, allowing the bearing races to drop smoothly into position without creating mechanical fatigue.

Subcontractor Validation

Hand the frame to the certified independent TIG welder. Have them reinforce the steering geometry by adding custom chromoly sleeves to the top and bottom of the neck tube. Document their AWS Certification ID in shop records to satisfy future insurance underwriting.

Component Assembly
  • WP factory-level motorcycle suspension triple-clamps
  • Lewis EP8+ 8-piston brake calipers
  • Select electric powertrain per tier specification (see Phase 3)
Phase 3
Dual Powertrain Strategy

The fleet is divided into two distinct tiers to balance extreme performance with optimized manufacturing costs.

Tier 1 — Hyper Models
Liquid-Cooled
Motor: Dual 50kW liquid-cooled motors — 100kW / 134HP combined Performance: 100–120+ MPH · 0–60 in 3.6 seconds Cooling: Closed-loop liquid-cooling radiator + electric pump — prevents magnetic breakdown and thermal drop at speed Target: Supercar-level performance benchmark
Tier 2 — Production Commuter
Air-Cooled
Motor: Single 27kW (~36HP) air-cooled motor Controller: EBMX X9000 or ASI BAC8000 FOC smart controller Thermal Protection: Hard automatic thermal derating limits — controller dials back phase current at temperature threshold, eliminating battery fire risk Financial Advantage: Eliminating cooling system saves $1,500–$2,500 per unit — direct margin improvement
The Financial Win

The Tier 2 air-cooled architecture saves $1,500–$2,500 per bike by eliminating the cooling system entirely. Smart controller thermal derating protects the motor without hardware cost. This difference is direct margin on every production unit.

Phase 4
Off-Road Demos & Content Engine

Generate organic buzz and validate the vehicle's mechanics completely outside the jurisdiction of federal road authorities.

  • Private Track Boundary: Keep the initial prototype exclusively on private property, closed testing facilities, or designated off-road parks. Under federal law, NHTSA has zero legal jurisdiction over non-highway vehicle operations.
  • The Street Ban: The prototype is never driven on public roadways. Operating an un-VIN'ed, unregistered 100 MPH vehicle on public asphalt risks immediate vehicle seizure and heavy corporate penalties.
  • Viral Marketing Assembly: Film every step of the high-end build — thermal bearing installation, 8-piston calipers, WP inverted forks, and raw silent track footage of 0–60 launches. These assets are the primary marketing engine.

Phase 5
Risk-Free Pre-Order Fundraising

Collect clean, legally compliant capital from buyers to fully fund the upcoming 30-bike street-production run.

Legal Distinction — Critical

Do not issue standard sales receipts or sell the physical prototype. A street-legal bike cannot be sold until it possesses a federal VIN. The mechanism below is legally compliant at every step.

The Contract Mechanism

Sell "Production Queue Reservation Slots" using a formal reservation agreement — not a sale of goods.

  • Deposit Structure: Collect a refundable deposit of $500–$1,000 to secure a chronological position on the future assembly line.
  • Mandatory Disclaimer Clause: Website and reservation contract must state: "Your deposit reserves a production assembly sequence number for a future street-legal vehicle. Final vehicle delivery is subject to federal and state manufacturing certifications."
Phase 6
Transitioning to 30-Bike Street Production

Once customer deposit cash is secured in the business bank account, immediately trigger the street-legal manufacturing timeline.

  • SAE Registry: Submit to SAE International to secure a unique 3-digit World Manufacturer Identifier (WMI) code to generate legal 17-digit VINs.
  • NHTSA Filing: Register the operating LLC as a low-volume motorcycle manufacturer under 49 CFR Part 566. File Part 565 VIN Decipherment Guide.
  • Bulk Importation: Order first batch of 30 production chromoly frames from China, clearing through a customs broker using standard entry bonds.
  • Self-Certification: Take the first finished production bike to a closed lot. Use a GPS data logger to film and record high-speed emergency stops to document compliance with FMVSS No. 122 (Motorcycle Braking) — bypasses expensive testing labs at no cost.
  • Insurance Activation: Hand the welder's AWS sign-offs, braking data, and controller thermal safety specs to a specialized surplus lines insurance broker to bind the product liability policy.
  • DMV Rollout: Print official Manufacturer Certificates of Origin (MCO) stamped with custom VINs. Hand MCOs to the 30 buyers so they can walk into any DMV and secure a clean, street-legal license plate.
The Capital Flywheel

Customer deposits fund the compliance process. Compliance enables street-legal delivery. Delivery triggers final payment. Final payments fund the next production run. The business self-funds from the first 30 units forward — investor capital goes to the prototype and the launch, not ongoing production.

L = Dual 50kW Motors (Liquid-Cooled)

12 – 14 Low-Volume Production Identifier [Your 3-Digit Low-Volume Code] — Assigned by SAE International alongside WMI to legally designate a manufacturer building under 500 units per year.
15 – 17 Sequential Production Number 001 to 999 — Chronological build order number off the assembly line. Bike #1 = 001. Bike #30 = 030.

Positions 12–14 (Low-Volume Production Identifier) are legally required to be assigned by SAE International alongside your WMI. Submit your LLC registration to SAE International to receive both codes before NHTSA filing. Once assigned, this VIN structure is stamped on the right side of the head tube of every production unit and printed on the Manufacturer Certificate of Origin (MCO) handed to each buyer.
