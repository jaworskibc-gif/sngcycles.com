The Problem

The Bike For The Guy The E Market Forgot.

The electric moto market got built around the beginner. Lighter. Smaller. Simpler. Safe for someone who isn't sure yet. Nobody built one for the rider who's been riding for twenty years, knows exactly what a real machine feels like, and wasn't going to apologize for wanting that in an electric platform. That's who this is for.

Wrong Geometry Compact platforms weren't built for you. They were sized for the average buyer and priced for the average wallet. If you're 6'1" throwing a leg over something with a 32" seat height, you feel the misfit in the first thirty seconds — and it never stops bothering you.
Wrong Visual Presence A serious rider on a compromised platform looks wrong. The machine should have presence before it moves. If you have to explain why the size is "actually fine for what it is," the machine already failed you.
Wrong Component Logic The brakes, suspension, and wheels are where the manufacturer's real opinion of you shows up. Economy choices on a premium price point aren't oversights — they're signals. SNG takes a different position on every component that matters.
Wrong Buyer Signal Most electric builds are marketed to gadget shoppers and first-time riders. The SNG buyer already owns a $20,000 machine and knows exactly what he's comparing it to. That buyer requires a completely different standard — and a brand that understands it without being told.
The Machine

One Platform. No Apologies.

Not a spec sheet assembled to hit a price point. Every decision in this build was made by a KTM master mechanic who would know immediately if it was wrong — and restarted the versions that were.

Rider Geometry

Adult Stance

39.5" seat height. 56.5–57" wheelbase. Warp 9 wheels — 21" front, 18" rear. These numbers aren't upgrades. They are what the platform was designed around from day one. A full-size adult sits on this machine and it looks right immediately.

Premium Hardware

The Component Stack

WP XACT PRO 7548 Cone Valve 48mm fork. YSS progressive mono rear. Lewis 6-piston front, 8-piston rear. Every component gets the same question: is this what a serious machine actually uses? If not, it doesn't go on.

Platform Logic

The Structure Earns It

CNC billet main frame. Wide chromoly subframe. Titanium hardware at critical connections. Engineered for the loads a full-size machine actually generates — not scaled down from something built for a lighter, cheaper platform.

Mechanical Credibility

No Renders. No Vaporware.

The machine in the garage is the machine. Not a concept. Not a prototype still three years from production. The 270E exists, it runs, and it was built by someone who would know if anything on it wasn't right.

Engineering Proof

The Hard Way Was The Only Way.

No factory funding. No VC. No shortcut. The founder is a KTM-certified master mechanic — which means when a version of this machine wasn't right, he was the first to know it, and the one who took it apart.

Some prototype cycles got close. Close wasn't the standard. Some got torn back down and restarted before they were ever shown to anyone — because showing a version that isn't right is worse than showing nothing. The 270E is the version that cleared every one of those gates. The numbers here are what that process cost and what it produced.

The Founder

He Didn't Wait For Someone Else To Build It.

Bryan Jaworski. Royal Palm Beach, Florida. KTM-certified master mechanic. He did not start SNG Cycles because the market needed a new brand — he started it because the machine he wanted didn't exist and he was the one person in the right position to fix that.

The build didn't start as a business plan. It started as a problem he couldn't solve by buying something available. The geometry was wrong on everything he could afford. The components were wrong. The stance was wrong. So he went into a real garage in South Florida and spent the next several years and over $50,000 of his own money doing something about it.
Every prototype that didn't earn its place got torn down. Not archived. Not displayed as a "learning version." Torn down and restarted. That is what it looks like when the standard isn't negotiable — and it is exactly what makes the buyer's money mean something when he puts it on the line.
